\chapter*{Abstract}

Environmental contamination by priority compounds requires the integration of
information on chemical structures, biological functions, degradation
pathways, toxicity, and institutional references. This information remains
distributed across databases with distinct observational units and curation
criteria, which hinders the interpretation of functional profiles derived from
omics data. BioRemPP constitutes a compound-oriented computational ecosystem
that integrates this content and provides different forms of querying and
analysis. The ecosystem comprises four components: the curated BioRemPP
database, the BioRemPP Database Explorer, the BioRemPP Web Service, and a
command-line interface. The database integrates the BioRemPP central table,
HADEG, the KEGG Degradation subset, and ToxCSM through relationships among
KEGG Orthology groups (KOs), compounds, EC numbers, reactions, pathways,
enzyme activity descriptors, institutional references, and toxicity
predictions. A Snakemake workflow automates its construction, while checks
anchored in KEGG relationships and structural and regression validations with
Great Expectations assess the consistency of the generated artifacts. The
Database Explorer provides five exploration modules and eight guided analyses
accompanied by versioned SQL queries. The Web Service receives sets of KOs
organized by sample and comprises 56 use cases distributed across eight
modules, including descriptive, multivariate, relational, toxicological, and
functional complementarity analyses. The command-line interface provides the
same operations for programmatic execution and integration into automated
pipelines. Version 1.1.0 of the database comprises 384 compounds distributed
across 12 chemical classes, 1,543 KOs, 994 EC numbers, 1,939 KEGG reaction
identifiers, 1,517 gene symbols, and 206 enzyme activity descriptors. Nine
institutional reference codes define the scope of priority compounds, and 370
compounds have predictions for 31 toxicological endpoints. The components
preserve KO and CPD identifiers, record software versions and analysis
parameters, and provide exportable tables, visualizations, and artifacts.
BioRemPP supports \textit{in silico} screening, functional profile comparison,
and hypothesis selection for subsequent studies. Associations based on KO
presence and predicted toxicity values represent functional potential and
computational context but do not replace transcriptomic, proteomic,
metabolomic, biochemical, or environmental validation.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} bioinformatics; bioremediation; functional
